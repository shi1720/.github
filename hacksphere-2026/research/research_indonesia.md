# FACT SHEET — Indonesian Smallholder Commodity Farming & EUDR Compliance
### Research dossier for hackathon proposal: Web3 + AI for EUDR export compliance
**Compiled:** September 14, 2026. All sources accessed 2026-09-14 unless noted. Figures verified by web search; conflicting figures are flagged inline and in Section 8.

**EUDR status as of today:** Regulation (EU) 2023/1115, amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 (December 2025). Application date: **December 30, 2026** for large/medium operators; **June 30, 2027** for micro and small enterprises (second delay, plus simplifications: one annual due-diligence statement covering expected volumes, simplified single declaration for micro/small primary operators, DDS submitted only by first operator placing goods on the EU market) [1][2]. In the first country benchmarking list (May 22, 2025) **Indonesia is classified "standard risk"** — not high risk, not low risk (only Belarus, DPRK, Myanmar, Russia are high risk) [3][4]. EUDR requires plot-level geolocation for every plot of origin (point for <4 ha; polygon for ≥4 ha), proof of production on land not deforested after Dec 31, 2020, and legality under producer-country law [1][5].

**2025–26 EUDR market news (coffee):**
- No evidence found of EU buyers *pausing* Indonesian coffee purchases; the opposite: EU purchases accelerated ahead of the deadline (above) [9][10]. USDA explicitly links the surge to EUDR preparedness of Indonesian exporters [9].
- **Price effect benchmark:** buyers are paying a premium of **~US$50/tonne for EUDR-compliant robusta** over conventional (reported for Vietnam, the closest comparator; only 35–40% of Vietnam's coffee area currently meets EUDR traceability) [18].
- Fairtrade International postponed the deadline for certified coffee co-ops to hold geolocation data for full membership from Jan 2026 to **January 2027** — evidence co-ops (incl. Indonesian ones) could not map members in time; it launched the free "Plot Insights" geodata tool in June 2026 [19][20].
- Analysts warn of a **two-tier market**: non-compliant volumes must find non-EU buyers at discounts (foodnavigator; Mongabay commentary estimates physical-segregation logistics add **up to +25% transport/storage cost**) [21][22].

## 3. COCOA & RUBBER (brief)

**Cocoa**
- Production 2024: **617,112 t** (2023: 632,117 t; peak 837,918 t in 2010); world's **7th** producer (~4% global) [37].
- Smallholders: **99.8% of national cocoa bean production** [37]; **>1.4 million smallholder farming households**, >90% of ~1.6M ha; farms 0.5–2 ha [38][39].
- Exports 2024: **US$2.6B** (348,092 t), mostly processed (cocoa butter 64.6% of value). Top destinations: **India 19.9%, Malaysia 15.6%, China 9.9%, US 9.0%, Russia 5.0%** — direct EU share small, but EU matters via grindings/butter (2023 cocoa+preparations exports US$1.20B; Estonia in top-5 for beans) [37][40].
- e-STDB coverage for cocoa farmers: **0.00%** (July 2024, MoA) [28].

**Rubber**
- Smallholders: **~85% of plantations / ~91% of production**; smallholder planted area **3.25–3.47M ha** (2023) [41][42].
- **~2.1–2.2 million rubber farming households**, ~90% smallholders (ministry data cited May 2026) [43].
- EU exports ~**US$1B/yr** (natural rubber; EUDR-covered) [44]; Gapkindo (Assoc. of Indonesian Rubber Companies) reported export volumes to EUDR-problematic destinations already falling going into 2025 [45].
- e-STDB coverage for rubber farmers: **0.03%** [28].

## 4. TRACEABILITY GAP — EVIDENCE BASE

**Registration / geolocation / land title**
- National e-STDB coverage across the four smallholder commodities is **≤1.07%** (palm) and effectively **zero** for coffee, cocoa, rubber (MoA via Jakarta Post, July 2024 data) [28][5]. The STDB is the government's own legality+geolocation instrument (it records the plot polygon) — so the STDB gap ≈ the mapping gap.
- EFI/Terpercaya: 15,054 e-STDB vs 2.4M palm households (Dec 2023) [24]; regional example: Pangkalan Banteng district (C. Kalimantan) 2024 — **1,280 of 13,042 farm operators (9.8%)** hold STDB [46].
- KOLTIVA (Dec 2025): of the 40%+ of palm land under smallholders, "**most remain unregistered and untraceable**" [27].
- Barriers stack (KOLTIVA SHINES program, 600+ smallholders, Kutai): (1) no geolocation data or formal land registration; (2) unclear tenure/overlapping claims; (3) limited digital literacy and poor internet; (4) produce mixing at collection points breaks chain of custody; (5) plots inside forest-zone classifications [25].
- Parak/agroforestry coffee plots in W. Sumatra: "**no polygons, not recorded in any database**" (ANTARA, Aug 2026) [47].
- CIPS/Southern Voice (2025): farmers "often lack digital tools or skills to systematically collect and report" geodata; **EUDR risks jeopardising livelihoods of ~98% of smallholder coffee farmers** in Indonesia (& Uganda) [12].

**Cost of traditional certification/mapping (per farmer / per ha)**
- RSPO certification of independent smallholders (Riau, Amanah association — first Indonesian group certified): upfront cost **€86/ha**; group certification cost **€191–751 per farmer** depending on group size (348 → 54 farmers); certification produced up to 21% revenue gain but **−8% net income in year 1** [48][49].
- Physical segregation for EUDR adds up to **+25%** transport & storage cost (industry estimate) [22].
- (Benchmark for pitch: government/donor STDB pilots require field teams with GPS — Ditjenbun now allows third parties to do STDB mapping/data collection to speed it up [50].)

**Digital access among farmers**
- BPS Agricultural Census 2023 (ST2023): Indonesia has **28.19 million farmers**; only **46.84%** use any modern machinery or digital technology (incl. internet/smartphone) [51].
- Smartphone ownership: **68.65% of Indonesians** (BPS 2024) [52]; internet penetration 79.5% (APJII 2024) with persistent rural gap [52]; rural household mobile-phone ownership 54.31% vs 69.53% urban (BPS 2020) [53].
- Farmer age structure: only 21.93% of farmers are 19–39 ("millennial") [51] — older farmers dominate, compounding digital-literacy constraints [54].

## 5. INDONESIAN GOVERNMENT RESPONSE

- **The system's exact name:** **National Dashboard of Indonesian Sustainable Commodities Data and Information** ("Dasbor Nasional Data dan Informasi Komoditas Berkelanjutan Indonesia") — commonly "**National Dashboard**." NOT "INA-Trace" (no such Indonesian system found; INATrace is an unrelated open-source EU project). Run by a National Dashboard Secretariat under the **Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs (CMEA/Kemenko Perekonomian)** — not Ministry of Trade/KADIN. Announced/soft-launched **September 2024**, expanded through 2025 for the Dec 2025 (now Dec 2026) EUDR deadline [28][55][56].
- Coverage design: **palm oil, coffee, cocoa, rubber, timber**, described as **blockchain-based**; integrates SIPERIBUN (company plantation licensing) + **e-STDB** (smallholder registration) + KLHK 2020 forest-cover maps; positioned as a **verification & integration layer** whose outputs EU-side buyers can check ("functions as a verification and integration system" — Diah Suradiredja, dashboard coordinator, Apr 2025) [28][56].
- **Documented limitations:** neither SIPERIBUN nor e-STDB uses the EUDR's Dec 31 2020 cut-off; smallholder registration near-zero (Sec. 2/4); >3M ha of palm in forest areas with unresolved legality; company self-reported data quality issues (Jakarta Post op-ed, Sept 2024) [28]. NGO Kaoem Telapak built independent monitor "Ground Truthed.id" to check it [56].
- **e-STDB acceleration:** Ministry of Agriculture (Ditjenbun) made e-STDB the main EUDR legality/traceability vehicle; opened data collection to third parties (buyers, NGOs, processors) to speed issuance (Doris Monica Sari Turnip, Ditjenbun, Aug 2026) [50]; Team Europe/GIZ funded a WRI-run e-STDB acceleration project Aug–Dec 2025 [57].
- **Joint Task Force:** **Ad Hoc Joint Task Force on EUDR (EU–Indonesia–Malaysia)**, agreed June 2023 (Aug 4, 2023 announcement); 5 workstreams incl. traceability, smallholders, benchmarking, forest maps; 2nd meeting Feb 2024 (Putrajaya), 3rd meeting Sept 12, 2024 (Brussels) — agreed to produce practical smallholder traceability guides; CPOPC 2025 ministerial backed its continuation; NGO Fern says it "must step up its game" on smallholder inclusion [58][59][60].
- **Official statements on smallholder readiness:** Deputy Foreign Minister **Arief Havas Oegroseno** (Mar 20, 2025): on EUDR Art. 10 sanction-screening duties — "**Can farmers do it? Of course not.**"; questioned why Indonesian farmers must hand over geodata the EU itself protects domestically [61]. Coordinating Minister **(then) Luhut Pandjaitan/Airlangga era** framing: EUDR = "**regulatory imperialism**" (June 2023) [62]; government position: EUDR "discriminatory toward small farmers" [62]. Indonesia's own estimate: **up to US$7B of exports at risk** (Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs, 2023) [44].
- **Trade-policy context:** EU–Indonesia **CEPA** concluded Sept 23, 2025 (signing/ratification track 2026) — raises the cost of EUDR failure and the payoff of compliance [63].

## 6. ECONOMIC STAKES

- **Total EU–Indonesia goods trade:** €28.9B (2025); EU imports from Indonesia €18.7B [64].
- **EUDR-commodity exposure:** Government (CMEA) figure: **up to US$7 billion** of Indonesian exports affected; components cited: **CPO to EU ~US$3.5B/yr, rubber ~US$1B, furniture ~US$0.6B** + coffee, cocoa [44]. (Cross-check: palm-to-EU US$2.1–2.4B in 2022–23 trade data [31] — the $3.5B figure includes derivatives/oleochemicals; flag as ministry estimate.)
- **Coffee exposure:** EU ≈16% of US$0.93–1.6B coffee exports ≈ **US$150–260M/yr** [12][8][9].
- **Diversion evidence:** EU palm imports fell >20% after EUDR adoption (2023/24 season) [65]; Indonesia's palm exports to EU −12.5% in value 2022→2023 [31]; Malaysia–China deal (Sept 2023) to double palm exports (US$533M) shows Asia-diversion channel [62]; Gapkindo reports rubber volumes shifting away from EU [45]; analysts and the ITC/USITC describe rerouting to **India/China/Pakistan**, and a **two-tier market** in which non-compliant product sells at a **discount** outside the EU while compliant product earns premiums (evidence point: **+US$50/t premium** for EUDR-compliant robusta, Vietnam, Sept 2026) [21][22][18][66].
- **Compliance-cost asymmetry:** €86/ha upfront certification (RSPO benchmark) [48] vs typical coffee-farm cash income on <1 ha — certification can consume a first-year income share large enough to produce net losses (−8% year-1) [48]; segregation +25% logistics [22].
- **If smallholders are excluded:** 98–99% of coffee/cocoa production and ~41% of palm area is smallholder-grown (Secs. 1–3) — exclusion cuts EU-bound supply chains off from most Indonesian origin volume, or forces exporters to source only from the ~1% mapped/certified minority [25][12]. CIPS/Southern Voice: welfare losses & rural income decline predicted; EPRC/CIPS headline: livelihoods of ~98% of smallholder coffee farmers at risk [12].

## 7. HUMAN-STORY MATERIAL (named, citable vignettes)

**(a) "Parak coffee and the missing coordinates" — Nagari Panti Selatan, Kabupaten Pasaman, West Sumatra.** ANTARA (Indonesian-language op-ed by Nofialdi, Andalas University community-service team, Aug 28, 2026). Minangkabau farmers grow coffee in ancestral *parak* agroforests — "tiered like a tidied forest" among areca, petai, banana and sugar-palm. Their system is the most forest-like coffee agriculture imaginable, yet the plots "have no polygons and are not recorded in any database," so under EUDR (polygon required ≥4 ha, coordinates for all) "the most forest-friendly coffee has the highest chance of being refused at Europe's door." Proposes: attach polygon maps to social-forestry permits, make geodata collection an official extension task, fund it publicly, and let cooperatives report collectively [47].

**(b) Koperasi Kopi Gayo Lues Bersinar — Gayo Lues, Aceh.** Local report (Gemarnews, Aug 4, 2026): with EU export rules tightening, the co-op runs field monitoring to verify every member plot, maps plantation locations against protected/conservation areas, sets aside HCV zones and builds origin-tracing — "We conduct this monitoring to ensure all members' garden areas have been identified" (co-op secretary). Illustrates co-ops doing EUDR mapping by hand, plot by plot [67].

**Supporting color:** The Gayo highlands (Aceh Tengah, Bener Meriah, Gayo Lues) hold Southeast Asia's largest Arabica area and an EU PGI since 2010 [16][17]; **Kopepi Ketiara** (Takengon) is a women-led Fairtrade co-op, ~2,000+ members across 19 villages, led by Rahmah — a ready-made protagonist cooperative for a demo narrative (its Fairtrade network faces the Jan 2027 geolocation deadline) [68][19]. AEKI Aceh chapter: European buyers "no longer want to accept coffee from forests," referencing EUDR [69]. WRI Indonesia runs a "Sustainable Gayo Coffee Project" on legality/traceability in these districts [70]. National webinar (Kehati/TFCA-Sumatera & SCOPI, Aug 5, 2026): "Implementing traceability is no longer just an option, but a necessity for the future of the Indonesian coffee industry" (Samedi); Java Kirana's founder on farmers' reality: "farmers cannot yet view the EUDR from a buyer's perspective, because for them, even meeting their daily needs remains a challenge" [50].

## 8. CONFLICTS, CAVEATS & PREMISE CHECK

**Figures that conflict (use with care):**
1. Coffee households: 1.63M (Ditjenbun 2022) vs 1.9M (MoA via CIPS 2025) [11][12].
2. Coffee production 2024: 813,345 t (BPS) vs ~720,000 t (AEKI) vs USDA MY bags [6][8][9].
3. Coffee export value 2024: US$1.63–1.64B (BPS) vs US$1.40B (AEKI) [13][8].
4. EU share of coffee exports: 16% (2023, trade data) vs ">30% of export value linked to Europe" (industry guide) [12][15].
5. Smallholder palm area: 6.2M ha vs 6.7M ha vs 6.94M ha (definitions: independent vs all smallholder incl. plasma) [24][25][29].
6. ISPO smallholder rate: "0.3% of smallholders" (GAPKI) vs 60,236 ha ≈0.9% of area vs "<0.1% certified" (older SPOC piece) — all agree on "<1%" [29][71].
7. Palm-to-EU value: US$2.1B (2023 trade data) vs US$3.5B/yr (CMEA incl. derivatives) [31][44].

**Evidence that cuts against the pitch premise ("smallholders largely unmapped and at risk of EU exclusion") — disclose honestly:**
- **EU buying is currently up, not down:** coffee shipments to Europe +72% in 2025/26 [9]; palm to EU +11.1% (Jan–Apr 2026) [34]. Front-loading and exporter preparedness mean no EU purchase-pause has materialized as of Sept 2026; the crunch arrives with enforcement from Dec 30, 2026.
- **The deadline has slipped twice** (2024→2025→2026/27) and simplifications reduced burdens (annual DDS; micro/small producers file one simplified declaration); a third slip or further softening is possible — Mongabay/commentators note political pressure continues [1][2][22].
- **Indonesia is "standard risk," not high risk** [3], and mainstream commentary argues big coffee companies are readier than they claim [72] — the binding constraint is the *smallholder data gap*, not country-level risk.
- **Mapping is not technically hard or per-se expensive** — a smartphone GPS point suffices for plots <4 ha; the bottlenecks are legality documents (STDB prerequisites incl. land title), institutional processing capacity in districts (FORTASBI: "the biggest challenge lies in institutional and governance issues at the regional level" [30]), aggregation/chain-of-custody, and trust — which is precisely the gap a Web3+AI registry pitch should target rather than "farmers can't be mapped."
- Fairtrade already ships a free geolocation tool (Plot Insights) [20]; KOLTIVA has 178,000+ producers registered [27] — a new solution must differentiate (e.g., farmer-owned data/verifiable credentials vs. platform lock-in).
